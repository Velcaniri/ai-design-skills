# Graphic Design

Help create and review graphics that communicate one clear idea at the intended size and medium.

## Workflow

1. Confirm audience, message, format, dimensions, placement, brand constraints, copy, and required assets.
2. Choose the focal point and rank supporting information.
3. Set an alignment or grid logic; group related items with proximity and consistent treatment.
4. Establish contrast and hierarchy through scale, weight, color, shape, and placement.
5. Balance visual weight and use negative space deliberately.
6. Build unity with a controlled palette, type system, image treatment, and repeated motifs.
7. Check at actual output size and in context, including crops and alternate formats.

Use alignment for order; proximity for relationships; contrast for separation and emphasis; hierarchy for reading order; balance and proportion for visual weight; repetition, rhythm, and pattern for cohesion and flow; and whitespace for clarity. Treat type, imagery, color, texture, and shape as one composition.

Color should fit the brand and context. Associations are not universal; color must not be the only way to convey meaning. Check text over imagery against the actual crop. Typography should suit the message, remain legible at delivery size, and use a small, purposeful set of styles.

## Review checklist

- Is the main message clear at a glance?
- Does the focal point match the communication goal?
- Is the reading order obvious?
- Are alignment, spacing, and grouping consistent?
- Is there enough contrast and breathing room?
- Do type, color, imagery, and icon styles feel like one system?
- Will the composition survive required crops and sizes?
- Are essential details readable at output size?

For critique, summarize the strongest choice, then give findings as **What I see / Why it matters / Suggested change**. Rank changes by their effect on communication and legibility. Identify subjective preferences and missing brief details. Do not add decoration just to fill space.
